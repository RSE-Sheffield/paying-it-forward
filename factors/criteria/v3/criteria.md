Research Data
We follow the definitions and examples of research data as defined by the UKRI’s research data policy.
Definition
Research data is ‘defined as recorded information that is collected, created, generated, observed or derived from existing sources through any research method, and used to support, validate or inform research findings’.
Examples of Research Data

creative practice process data and analysis materials
digitised records and digital representations of physical objects,
image, sound and video files,
interview transcripts and survey data,
manuscript translations,
measurements or observations from experiments or fieldwork,
model and simulation inputs and outputs
software-generated outputs
statistics
synthetic data
model weights

Examples of Not Research Data

Literature and Papers
Physical Samples
Protocols

Research Software
We are using the current definitions of research software from the UKRI research data policy.
Definition
Research software is source code files, algorithms, scripts, computational workflows, documentation and executables that were created during the research process or for a research purpose.
Examples of Research Software
Source code
Packages
Scripts
Applications
Model implementations
Examples of Not Research Software

Widely established software used for general tasks (Microsoft Excel or Google docs)
Survey software (e.g. SurveyMonkey, Qualtrics)
Generic statistical software (e.g. STATA)
Generic Data processing software (e.g. Excel, Databricks, Origin)
Generic plotting software (e.g. matplotlib)

Paying it Forward Taxonomy

We assign a grade for both research software and data to determine whether an artifact has been generated, used or not relevant by the research. This judgement is made from a research paper. This taxonomy is illustrated in figure 1 with exact definitions below.


Definitions


Definition
S1 Research Software Generated
The paper claims to have created or significantly extended research software.
S2 Research Software Used
The paper claims to have used existing research software or there is insufficient evidence that they have created or significantly extended.
S3 No Research Software
The paper does not claim to have either created or used research software. If software has only been used as a utility (such as processing or plotting data) but is not claimed as a contribution in its own right, then this is also S3.
D1 Research Data Generated
The paper claims to have created or significantly extended research data. This includes significantly restructuring existing data. This does not include findings or statistics derived from a data source.
D2 Research Data Used
The paper claims to have used existing research data. This includes if the research has findings or statistics are derived from a data source.
D3 No Research Data
The paper does not claim to have either created or used research data.


General Rules

If a paper both uses and generates software/data, then it belongs in the generated category. In other words, we assume if software/data is generated, it is also used.

If the appropriate category is unclear, require clear evidence before classifying software or data as generated. Where the paper does not clearly establish that the software or data was produced by the research, classify it as used instead.

The definitions of research data and software are independent of whether they have been shared or published. For example, if the paper states research software or data has been generated, but doesn't provide a way of accessing it, or even if they’ve published it, then we still consider this S1 or D1. This includes statements like ‘data available on request’. Future Work packages will assess the data stewardship/fairness of the research.

Artefacts must have been used or created in the research reported in the paper. Planned or proposed artefacts are not considered. For example, statements such as “We will use software X” or “We will collect data Y” should not be classified as evidence of software or data use/generation. This is particularly common in research protocols; such papers should be classified as S3D3 unless they provide evidence of actual software or data use/generation.

The unit of analysis is a research paper.
